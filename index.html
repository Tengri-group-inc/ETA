The Thesis

Most raw land in the United States is functionally unbuildable on day one. Soil won't perc. Wells aren't sited. Access isn't engineered. Permits aren't planned. The buyer doesn't know what they don't know.

The work of turning that land into a build-ready, financeable parcel is fragmented across a dozen specialists: Sewage Enforcement Officers, soil scientists, well drillers, excavators, civil engineers, surveyors, environmental consultants, township planners. Each one operates as a small, undercapitalized, schedule-constrained business. None of them coordinate. The buyer eats the integration cost.

We think there is a generational opportunity to consolidate this stack into a single operating platform that delivers buildability decisions in days instead of months, owns the bottleneck capabilities through acquisition and in-house build, and finances the projects it underwrites.

That's what Tengri Group is building.

The full stack is a 5-year build. We don't pretend otherwise. We are operating at Layer 1 today, with an active acquisition program targeting Layers 2 and 3.

For Investors

Tengri Group is an operator-led acquisition platform. The model is entrepreneurship through acquisition (ETA), not private equity, not a real estate syndication, not a search fund LP vehicle. The founder is the searcher, the buyer, and the operator. Capital partners participate at the deal or platform level on a non-operational basis.

Why the category fits the ETA playbook
  • Hundreds of sub-$5M Pennsylvania-based septic, drilling, excavation, and small civil engineering businesses: fragmented, owner-operated, often retiring with no succession plan.
  • Recurring revenue, durable customer demand, low technology disruption risk.
  • Standard SBA 7(a) and 504 financing available for clean targets.
  • Operational improvement (marketing, scheduling, back-office) creates real margin without raising prices.
Typical acquisition structure
  • 70–80% SBA-backed senior debt
  • 10–15% seller note where appropriate
  • 10–20% investor equity, non-operational, non-controlling
  • Founder co-invests in every transaction
Two ways to participate
  • Deal-level co-investment. Specific acquisitions where the target has been identified, financials reviewed, and SBA pre-qualification underway. Investor checks $50K–$300K per deal. Hold horizon 5–10 years.
  • Platform-level commitment. Exposure across multiple acquisitions over time. Capital deployed deal-by-deal as they close, with co-invest rights on follow-on opportunities.
What you should know
  • We are early. The first platform acquisition is in active sourcing.
  • We do not guarantee returns. Anyone who does is lying.
  • We invest our own capital in everything we ask others to invest in.
  • We send quarterly written updates. We answer the phone.
  • We do not pay placement fees. We do not work with promoters.
On the approach

The acquisition approach here comes directly from the ETA framework Aidar is studying at Cornell's Johnson Graduate School of Management. He's the one running the businesses, not a financial sponsor with a portfolio CEO in between.
